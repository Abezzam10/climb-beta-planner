What this does and does not know

  • It works in flat 2D. It cannot see wall angle, depth, hold shape, chalk, or friction in a photo.
  • "Reach" comes from rough body proportions: arm reach is about 39% of wingspan, leg reach about 50% of height. Real people vary a lot.
  • Each move must keep the hips within 30% of your height of the previous pose, and the three limbs that stay put must still be within reach halfway through. That is a simple 2D check, not real physics.
  • "Balance" is how far the hips drift sideways from the feet. It is not a real torque calculation.
  • "Dynamic" moves are reaches slightly past the static limit. They are flagged, not checked for safety.
  • The ground is only where you set the ground line. If you do not set one, feet must start on marked holds.
  • Hold types and sizes come from you, or from the sample wall. Nothing is detected automatically yet.
  • It does not know your strength or grade, and cannot say a move is possible, safe, or "optimal". Use your own judgment and climb with proper safety practices.

About this project

An experiment in turning a wall photo and a few marked holds into a suggested order of hand and foot moves, using simple reach and balance rules. It is an early prototype: it says plainly what it is guessing and does not claim a route is best, safe, or possible. Planned later work: automatic hold detection, and offline experiments to improve the search, tested on walls it was not tuned on.

Everything runs in your browser and works offline once loaded. Photos are not uploaded anywhere.
